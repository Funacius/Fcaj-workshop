## Event Objectives

- Introduce the internship learning journey and connect participants with the
  AWS community.
- Share practical career stories from cloud, AI, DevOps, data, and process
  engineering speakers.
- Help participants understand how technical skills, communication, and
  workplace culture are connected in real projects.
- Provide guidance on learning paths, community involvement, and preparing for
  future technology careers.

## Key Highlights

### DevOps engineering and technical fundamentals

Trong H. Truong shared what a DevOps Engineer actually does in real teams. The
session emphasized that DevOps is not only about tools, but also about
understanding how applications run, how systems fail, and how teams build,
deploy, monitor, and improve software.

- Important fundamentals: Linux, networking basics, programming, Git, CI/CD,
  containers, logs, configuration, and environment variables.
- A useful learning mindset: build small projects, deploy them, monitor them,
  break them, and fix them.
- DevOps work requires communication and system thinking, not only command-line
  execution.

### From First Cloud AI Journey to AWS community opportunities

Danh Hoang Hieu Nghi presented a journey from First Cloud AI Journey to broader
AWS community and partner opportunities. This part helped me understand that
learning AWS is not limited to completing labs; it can also lead to community
activities, networking, public sharing, and professional growth.

- AWS Community Builder and AWS Student Builder programs encourage learners to
  contribute knowledge back to the community.
- LinkedIn networking, personal branding, and consistent learning are valuable
  for long-term career development.
- Community participation can help students move from passive learning to active
  contribution.

### Multinational company culture and recruitment mindset

Dat Pham and Cuong Nguyen shared practical stories about working culture in
multinational environments. The session discussed recruitment processes,
business culture, communication, and professional standards.

- Recruitment often includes resume screening, recruiter interviews, technical
  or logical tests, and behavioral interviews.
- The STAR model is useful for explaining real experience clearly.
- Modern engineering culture encourages no-blame post-mortems, inclusive
  communication, and continuous improvement.
- Vietnamese engineers need both technical ability and global working standards
  to participate confidently in international environments.

## Key Takeaways

### Technical knowledge

- I gained a clearer understanding of the foundation needed before using
  advanced DevOps tools: Linux, networking, programming, Git, CI/CD, containers,
  logs, and deployment environments.
- I learned that cloud work requires the ability to connect multiple layers:
  application code, infrastructure, monitoring, configuration, and team
  operations.
- The event reinforced the importance of building real projects instead of only
  reading theory.

### Practical value

- The speaker stories helped me see how technical learning can connect to career
  preparation, community contribution, and professional communication.
- I learned that asking "why" before "how" is important when solving technical
  problems.
- The discussion about recruitment and MNC culture helped me understand how to
  present experience and prepare for interviews more effectively.

### Connection to EduCloud Lite

- The DevOps mindset influenced how I approached EduCloud Lite deployment:
  build, deploy, observe logs, identify root causes, and fix issues layer by
  layer.
- The emphasis on environment variables, logs, CI/CD, and application runtime
  directly connects to my later work with Elastic Beanstalk, Amplify Hosting,
  CloudFront, and production troubleshooting.
- The community-sharing mindset also influenced how I documented EduCloud Lite
  as a public workshop/report website.

## Applying to Work

- Apply DevOps fundamentals when deploying EduCloud Lite instead of treating
  deployment as a single upload step.
- Use logs and health checks to understand production problems instead of
  changing multiple settings blindly.
- Improve technical communication by documenting architecture, deployment steps,
  and troubleshooting notes clearly.
- Connect project implementation with community-style knowledge sharing through
  the Hugo report and workshop pages.

## Event Experience

Attending the First Cloud AI Journey Meetup was a valuable starting point in my
internship. The event gave me a broader view of what it means to learn cloud
computing seriously: not only studying AWS services, but also understanding
engineering habits, communication, community involvement, and workplace culture.

### Learning from speakers and participants

- The DevOps sharing helped me understand the daily responsibilities behind
  building and operating software systems.
- The AWS community sharing showed how students can grow by joining programs,
  publishing knowledge, and connecting with other learners.
- The MNC culture session helped me think more carefully about interview
  preparation, teamwork, and professional behavior.

### Hands-on or discussion experience

- I reviewed the event slide decks after the meetup to connect each topic with
  my own internship project.
- I used the ideas about deployment, logging, communication, and continuous
  learning as references while planning the EduCloud Lite implementation.

### Lessons learned

- Fundamentals matter more than memorizing individual tools.
- A good engineer needs to understand both the technical system and the people
  working around it.
- Cloud learning becomes more meaningful when it is connected to a real project,
  public documentation, and community discussion.
